# Build With XO — feedback essay (entrant's own words, pasted verbatim)

What I Built
For this bounty, I built EmberVault, a hashlock tip vault on XO running live on the Bitcoin Cash chipnet. The goal was to create a trustless way to send tips using smart contracts. In my setup, a funder locks a specific amount of BCH into the vault contract, and the beneficiary can claim those funds by revealing a secret passphrase—the preimage—alongside their cryptographic signature. To make sure the money never gets permanently stuck if the beneficiary disappears, I also added a time-locked refund path. Once a certain block height deadline passes, the original funder can reclaim their locked BCH. Everything works end-to-end on chipnet.
What Went Smoothly
Once I got started, the main workflow inside XO felt surprisingly smooth and intuitive. Usually, building UTXO smart contracts requires a lot of manual setup and boilerplate code, which can get messy fast. With XO, going from defining the contract template to generating an invitation and completing the signing flow was clean and logical. Whenever I needed to verify what my contract was actually doing on-chain, the debugging tools at blaze.xo.cash were a huge help. Being able to visually inspect raw transaction hexes, inputs, and outputs made it much easier to double-check my logic and confirm that the funds were moving through the right paths.
What Broke or Confused Me
Despite the smooth start, I ran into several frustrating bugs while testing edge cases. The biggest issue happened when I was testing funding transactions with change outputs. Everything looked fine at first because the transaction went through without throwing an error, but when I checked my balance afterward, I realized my change output had completely vanished. After digging into the transaction details, I discovered an XO engine bug where the change output generated empty bytecode, effectively burning the funds. I ended up losing around 1 million testnet sats across three separate incidents before I caught what was happening.
Testing the refund path was another confusing roadblock. I waited until the chain was 25 to 80 blocks past my contract deadline and broadcast what looked like a completely valid CLTV refund transaction, yet the chipnet node kept rejecting it with a "bad-txns-nonfinal" error. Early on, I also fell into a P2SH trap when I tried wrapping the script manually—it broke the signature check, and the generic error message gave no hint about why it was failing. On top of that, refilling my test wallet was a pain because the chipnet faucet requires a Proof-of-Work challenge and enforces a strict 5-hour cooldown, which stalled my testing every time sats got burned.
Suggestions and Closing
Overall, XO makes building smart contracts on Bitcoin Cash way more approachable than writing everything from scratch. My top suggestion for the team is to fix that empty bytecode change bug immediately so builders do not accidentally burn funds during development. Adding clearer, more specific error messages for P2SH wrapping and CLTV locktime mismatches would also save hours of debugging guesswork. Despite the rough edges I hit on chipnet, I genuinely enjoyed the workflow and will definitely use XO again for future BCH projects.
